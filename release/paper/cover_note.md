# Cover note: Does a Local MP Get a Local Bonus?

## Suggested journals

Original articles "no more than 9,000 words in length including all notes but excluding references"; "no fees payable to submit or publish". It published Campbell and Cowley (2014) and Childs and Cowley (2011) on local candidates. Fits: the main text is about 8,200 words before the references (including tables); move the appendix tables to an online supplement.
2. **British Journal of Politics and International Relations** (Sage, Political Studies Association). Original research articles "between 8-10,000 words, excluding the list of references"; no fees for standard publication. Fits as it stands, with the appendix as a supplement.
3. **Electoral Studies** (Elsevier). The natural home for long-run, data-heavy studies of elections and candidate effects (Collignon and Sajuria 2018 appeared there). A hybrid subscription journal: no charge on the subscription route, an APC only for optional open access. Its word limit could not be confirmed in this session; check the current guide for authors.

Also possible: **Parliamentary Affairs** (Oxford University Press; original articles "Maximum words: 8,000", no charge listed for the standard licence). It would need cutting: lead with H1, H2 and the time trend and move H4 to H6 to a supplement.

## Summary of the contribution (for a cover letter)

Voters say they prefer local candidates, and British parties have selected far more of them since 2010, but whether local roots win real votes over the long run has not been tested. This paper links every MP returned at a UK general election from 1832 to 2024 (31,737 returns) to their birthplace and to constituency results, and tests pre-specified hypotheses with six designs that compare MPs within the same constituency. MPs born within 25 km of their seat win about 1.3 percentage points more of the vote than other MPs elected for the same seat, net of party performance; the estimate is the same among first-time winners and within the same party and seat, survives alternative definitions of local and placebo tests, and is matched in sign by re-election, succession and retirement designs. Yet local MPs are no less likely to be defeated and serve no longer, because most margins dwarf the bonus, and the bonus has not grown as localness has risen. The paper reconciles experimental evidence of a preference for local candidates with the modest weight of roots at the ballot box.
